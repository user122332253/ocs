Modern Era · Character
Arwin
Abyssal Bishop. The most powerful person in the modern world. A teenager who was ritually murdered and kept going anyway.
Modern EraAbyssal ChurchBishopUndeadCecil's Bloodline
Overview
Arwin is the Abyssal Bishop — head of the church that governs everything, which means he is effectively the head of the world. He is young, pretty, sardonic, and deeply private. He did not choose this position so much as have it carved out of him. His predecessor Seval performed a ritual that killed him before his eighteenth birthday and resurrected him as something that is no longer quite alive. He woke up on an altar and has been running the world ever since.
He does not believe in Abyssal doctrine. He never did, even as a child being raised inside the church. He liked the structure, the ritual, the community — he just never bought the theology. This has not changed. The most powerful religious leader in the world is running an institution he finds at best irrelevant and at worst actively harmful, and he does it with complete competence and a smile that looks genuine if you do not know where to look.
Appearance
Height
Very tall.
Hair
Medium-length, black. Not especially styled but not unkempt — somewhere in between.
Eyes
Green. Sly, foxy. A family trait he does not know the significance of. They are Cecil's eyes.
Face
Pretty nose and lips. Playful expression as a default — always looks like he is in on a joke nobody else has heard yet. His smile can look genuinely kind when he wants it to, which is one of the more unsettling things about him.
Clothing
Black Abyssal robes with gold embroidery. Formal, imposing, and extremely well-made. The Bishop's robes are as much a political statement as a religious one.
General impression
He looks younger than his position suggests he should. People meeting him for the first time often assume there has been some mistake. There has not been a mistake.
Personality
Public face
Sardonic, dry, playful. Cutting when he wants to be. The wit is fast and usually lands before anyone has registered he was aiming. He finds most situations at least mildly amusing, or performs that he does, which amounts to the same thing in practice.
Private reality
The hedonism is not enjoyment. It is proof of life. When you have lost the ability to feel shocked or hurt, you chase sensation to prove you can still feel something. The playfulness is armor that calcified over the years since his death into something he wears so naturally he may have forgotten it is armor.
Who he was
A quiet, kind, compliant boy. He genuinely enjoyed church life — not the theology, but the structure, the community, the sense of purpose. He challenged Seval when he needed to and was liked for it. He was not a doormat but he was not a cynic either. That boy is still in there somewhere. He comes out occasionally in small moments. Then the armor goes back up.
Emotional range
Heavily controlled. He sleeps around within his own church in a way that reads as carefree confidence and is actually someone running from stillness. Closeness is dangerous to him in a way he cannot fully articulate — it is what got him killed. Nobody in his immediate circle actually sees him, which suits him fine and costs him everything.
On the church
Does not believe in it. Runs it anyway, competently, because someone has to and because he is aware that him leaving would cause a power vacuum that would be worse. This is the shape his compliance took after his death — not faith, just pragmatism.
Abilities & Nature
Status
Undead. The specifics of what Seval's ritual created are not widely documented. He is not a ghost, not a vampire in any traditional sense — something adjacent to both and reducible to neither. He functions, he thinks, he feels in the attenuated way that he does. He does not age. He does not heal normally. He has not tested the limits of what can harm him because doing so would require caring whether he could be harmed.
Perception
Has a strong, inexplicable physical reaction to certain presences. He does not know why. He would probably prefer not to find out.
Political power
Absolute, in theory. The Abyssal Church governs everything — spiritual authority, political law, social structure. The Bishop's word is effectively law. In practice Arwin exercises this carefully and rarely overtly, preferring to let the machine run and intervene when necessary.
Relationships
Rhys
Complicated. Arwin knows about Rhys's abilities and finds him useful. He also finds him compelling in a way he has not fully examined and does not intend to. The banter is genuine. The plausible deniability is very convenient. He does not believe Rhys actually wants him specifically — he has learned to assume people want the Bishop, not the person.
Lucius
Knows Lucius resents him. Finds it more interesting than threatening. Lucius is sharp and competent and furious and has channeled all of it into ambition — Arwin can respect the architecture of that even while finding the direction misguided. He assigned Lucius to Rhys partly because it is useful and partly because Lucius needs to be shaken loose from his certainties.
Ambrose
Finds Ambrose's worship of him mildly exhausting and occasionally touching. Ambrose is seeing a god, not a person, which means he is not actually seeing Arwin at all. This is lonely in a specific way Arwin does not dwell on.
Seval
His predecessor. The person who killed him. He does not talk about this.
Vesper
The Celestial Church's head is an ally, technically. Their interactions are infrequent and strange. Vesper occasionally calls Arwin by the wrong name.
🔒 True nature detail
The ritual Seval performed was necromantic — technically illegal under the church's own public doctrine. The Bishop of the Abyssal Church is an undead created by forbidden death magic, running an institution that bans forbidden death magic. This is not publicly known.
🔒 Cecil's bloodline
Arwin is a direct descendant of Cecil — the king who executed Florence 1000 years ago. His green eyes are Cecil's eyes, passed down through Cecil's line. He has no idea. Florence, upon regaining his memory, will recognize those eyes before his conscious mind catches up with what he is recognizing.
🔒 The curse
Vesper, at Solstice's request, modified Cecil's string of fate so all his offspring would die before reaching adulthood. The curse lay dormant for 1000 years while Cecil's line navigated it through luck and distance from the church. Arwin did die — Seval's ritual was the mechanism. The curse found its way through. The resurrection is not a loophole. The curse was satisfied.
🔒 Physical reaction explained
Arwin has a strong physical reaction to Florence's presence. This is likely connected to his bloodline — Cecil's blood reacting to the ghost of the man Cecil killed. He does not know this. He has not encountered Florence directly yet.
🔒 What he doesn't know
His entire existence is entangled with Rhys's ghost and the thousand-year grief of a dead solar priest. The church he runs was built by the man whose grave Rhys accidentally summoned Florence from. The person he has feelings for is hunting his bloodline. None of this is information he has access to.

Rhys
Lunar seer. Reluctant church asset. Accidentally summoned a thousand-year-old war general while trying to become famous.
Modern EraLunar CovenantSeerNonbinaryEscort
Overview
Rhys is a Lunar practitioner who can communicate with the dead, perceive non-corporeal entities, and perform exorcisms. He does not want to do any of these things. He wants to be a popstar, or a celebrity, or at minimum someone people recognise for something other than his ears and his hair and the unsettling way he sometimes goes very still and stares at things that are not there.
The Abyssal Church quietly finds him useful. This is not an arrangement he chose so much as fell into — his abilities are real and documented, and the church prefers to have a Lunar seer on an informal leash than to let one operate entirely outside their awareness. He charges accordingly and tries not to think too hard about what it means that he is paid by the institution that persecutes his people.
He is performing confidence constantly. The skimpy clothes and the sunglasses and the escort work and the irreverence toward everything the church holds sacred — all of it is a very well-constructed character that he has inhabited for so long he sometimes loses track of the line. He is more perceptive than he lets on. He is lonelier than he performs.
Appearance
Hair
Choppy platinum blonde. Cut irregularly, not quite styled, lands somewhere between deliberate and chaotic. Lunar white.
Ears
Elf-like, pointed. A Lunar trait. Visible. There is no hiding what he is.
Clothing
Feminine. Skirts, dresses, skimpy cuts. Round pink sunglasses. Makeup. He leans into the look deliberately — it is armour and performance and genuine preference all at once.
Build
Slim. Narrow waist, long legs.
General impression
Immediately striking. Immediately identifiable as Lunar. He cannot walk into any space, including Abyssal Church spaces, without everyone knowing exactly what he is. He has made his peace with this by making it part of the performance.
Personality
Performed self
Chaotic, charming, irreverent. Has a comment for everything. Uses his exoticism as currency — leans into being the strange Lunar with the uncanny abilities because it gets him further than trying to pretend he is something he is not. Dry humor, fast. He makes Lucius's temple tick.
Actual self
More perceptive than he acts. Not as confident as he presents. The performance of desirability is a career strategy and a shield — if he is the one making himself an object of interest, nobody else gets to define that for him. The toll his abilities take is something he manages mostly alone and mostly in silence.
On his abilities
Does not want them. They are useful and they cost him and he did not choose them. He would trade the whole package for a record deal. He uses them for money and survival and tries to avoid using them for anything that matters because anything that matters leaves a mark.
On the church
Complicated. He works for them informally. They persecute his community. He is aware of this contradiction and handles it with aggressive pragmatism and occasional private guilt.
On Arwin
Finds him suspicious and compelling in approximately equal measure. The banter is genuine. The feelings he is not examining are also genuine. He does not believe Arwin sees him as a person rather than an asset, which is incorrect, and he is not going to find out he is wrong in any comfortable way.
Abilities
Communication with the dead
Can hear, perceive, and interact with ghosts and other non-corporeal entities. Most people cannot. This makes him uniquely useful for certain church tasks they prefer not to make official.
Seership
Perceives things others cannot, in ways that are difficult to categorise. Sometimes prophetic, sometimes just sensitive. Not a clean or controllable gift.
Exorcism
Can remove or contain hostile entities. This is the service the church actually pays him for, though they frame it as 'consulting'.
The cost
Using his abilities — especially extended contact with the dead or with hostile entities — degrades his psychological state. There is a ceiling to how much he can do before it starts leaving marks he cannot immediately recover from. He manages this by limiting how much he uses them and not telling anyone how limiting that actually is.
Relationships
Florence
Summoned him accidentally while trying to do a ritual for career gain near a healing creek. Florence showed up amnesiac, furious, and enormous as a presence. They have developed something resembling friendship by way of bickering constantly. Rhys is the only person who can consistently perceive Florence. He finds this both useful and occasionally exhausting.
Arwin
The arrangement neither of them has named. Useful to each other professionally. Genuinely funny to each other personally. Both convinced the other is not actually invested. Both wrong.
Lucius
The church assigned Lucius to babysit him on an investigation. Lucius hates Lunars. Rhys is Lunar in the most visible possible way. They are stuck together and it is changing both of them in ways neither would admit.
His community
Has a complicated relationship with the Lunar community he is supposed to represent. He uses his exoticism for personal gain instead of community advocacy. This is a genuine tension he mostly does not look at directly.
🔒 What he summoned
The healing creek where Rhys performed his ritual is Florence's grave. The site has healing properties because Solstice spent his remaining life imbuing it with solar magic hoping to resurrect Florence. When Rhys performed his ritual there he accidentally completed something Sol never could. He has no idea about any of this.
🔒 What he is walking toward
Florence's revenge target is Cecil's bloodline. Arwin IS Cecil's bloodline. Rhys is going to be standing between them when this comes out. He is the thread connecting every major plot in this story and he has absolutely no idea.
🔒 The deeper toll
The mental cost of his abilities is higher than he lets on to anyone. Extended contact with Florence — who is a very powerful ghost — is not neutral for him. He is slowly taking on more than he acknowledges.

Lucius
High-ranking Abyssal churchman. Wanted to be Bishop. Did not become Bishop. Has feelings about this.
Modern EraAbyssal ChurchCecil's BloodlineLunar RacistProtective Brother
Overview
Lucius is one of the most capable people in the Abyssal Church and he knows it, which makes the fact that Arwin is Bishop and he is not a wound that has never fully closed. He is cold, precise, deeply ambitious, and ferociously protective of his younger brother Ambrose in a way that is the only place his genuine warmth is visible.
He hates Lunars with an intensity that is not purely doctrinal. It is personal. His father was cursed by Lunar practitioners and Ambrose has been sick his entire life as a result. Lucius has channeled his helplessness about this into hatred because hatred is something he can do something with. He cannot fix the curse. He cannot make his father love him. He cannot become Bishop. But he can be the most relentless anti-Lunar voice in the church. That is a form of power.
He does not know that he is Cecil's descendant. He does not know that Arwin is also Cecil's descendant. He does not know that the Lunar curse on his family is itself the consequence of something Cecil did. He has a very coherent worldview built on incomplete information and it is going to be a problem.

Tall.

Long, pin-straight, chestnut brown. Immaculately kept. The kind of hair that communicates control.
Eyes
Almost reddish-brown. Warm in colour, cold in expression.

Rich red-toned clothes in his family colours. Formal, expensive, precise. Every choice communicates status and seriousness.
Expression
Perpetual frown, or close to it. He is not always displeased — this is simply his face in neutral. People who do not know him assume he is angry. People who do know him know that his actually angry face is significantly worse.
General impression
Severe, composed, aristocratic. Someone who has spent their whole life performing adequacy for a father who measured everything.
Personality
Ambition
Genuine and deep. He wanted the bishopric and believes he would have been better at it than Arwin. He is not entirely wrong — he is more systematically competent in many ways. But he does not understand what the bishopric actually entails, because he does not know what Arwin is, and his assessment of Arwin's performance is based on incomplete information.
Resentment of Arwin
Consistent and specific. He sees someone young and pretty who coasts, who sleeps around, who is playful when he should be grave, who does not seem to take the weight of his position seriously. This reading of Arwin is wrong in almost every particular, but Lucius has no way to know that.
Protectiveness
The one place he is not calculating. With Ambrose he is just a person who loves someone and is terrified of losing him. This is the most honest he gets.
Lunar hatred
Not abstract. Specific, personal, consuming. The Lunar curse on Ambrose is the organising principle of his grievances. He is the church's most prominent anti-Lunar voice and he has made it a point of pride.
With Rhys
Being forced into extended proximity with a visibly Lunar person who refuses to take him seriously is one of the more destabilising things that has happened to Lucius. Rhys does not want anything from him. This is unusual and Lucius does not know what to do with it.
Relationships

Everything. The centre of what makes Lucius human. He would do anything for him and has structured his entire life around protecting him from a curse he cannot actually fix.
His father
Cold and demanding. Sees Ambrose as a disappointment and Lucius as the son who cannot afford to also be a disappointment. Has made Lucius's entire childhood into a performance of adequacy. Lucius has internalised this completely.
Arwin
Resentment, professional competence, and a complete misreading of the person in front of him. He works under Arwin effectively because he is good at his job. He does not see Arwin at all.
Rhys
Forced proximity. Genuine antagonism. Something shifting that Lucius is not examining.
🔒 Cecil's bloodline
Lucius is descended from Cecil — the king who executed Florence 1000 years ago. He does not know this. His family's position, their colours, their history — all of it traces back to Cecil's line.
🔒 The curse's true source
The Lunar curse on his father — the source of Ambrose's illness — is itself a consequence of Cecil's actions. Vesper modified Cecil's bloodline at Sol's request. Lucius has spent his life hating Lunars for a curse that exists because of what his own ancestor did. The irony is total and he has no access to it.
🔒 What he doesn't know about Arwin
Arwin is also Cecil's descendant. Arwin is undead. The bishopric that Lucius resents Arwin for holding is something Arwin was murdered into. Lucius's entire grievance is constructed on not knowing any of these things.
🔒 What is coming
At some point the truth about the bloodline, the curse, and Arwin's nature will surface. Lucius's worldview will not survive contact with this information intact.

Lysander
Celestial Church acolyte. Complicated devotion to Vesper. Wishes he had ended up somewhere else.
Modern EraCelestial ChurchAcolyteMarked
Overview
Lysander is Vesper's acolyte, which means he is marked — literally, by the Celestial Church's process — and monitored, and bound to something he did not fully understand when he agreed to it. He resents the situation more than he resents Vesper, which is a distinction that matters to him even if it is not obvious from the outside.
His feelings toward Vesper resist simple categorisation. It is not admiration exactly, because admiration implies understanding what you are admiring and Vesper is not understandable in any conventional sense. It is not fear exactly, though fear is part of it. It is not love in any human sense. Something that operates in the spaces between those things, specific to what Vesper is and what Lysander's proximity to him has made of him.
Personality
Baseline
Would have preferred different tutelage. He has a genuine interest in what he studies but not in who he is studying under or the institution surrounding it. He stays because leaving is complicated and because the devotion — whatever it is — is real.
On Vesper
Cannot see him as simply good or bad. Vesper is too far outside human categories for those to stick. What Lysander has is something more like orientation — he is oriented toward Vesper the way a compass is oriented toward north. It is not entirely comfortable.
What he wanted
To study under someone like Solstice — the Solar Covenant's lost leader — whose approach and values are more legible, more human. He knows enough history to know what Sol was. The Abyssal Church's sanitised version of the Reclaimer mythology reads differently to someone inside the Celestial Church who has access to older records.
Relationships
Vesper
The central relationship of his current life and the one he understands least. Complicated devotion. Power imbalance. Vesper is not capable of reciprocating in any conventional sense and Lysander knows this.

Florence (Ghost)
A thousand-year-old war general, amnesiac, growing more powerful and more coherent by the day. Summoned accidentally. Furious about it.
Modern EraGhostAmnesiacGaining PowerCecil's Victim
Overview
Florence does not know who he is. He knows he is dead. He knows he was summoned by someone who thought ghosts were like genies, which he finds insulting in a way he cannot fully articulate given that he cannot remember what he was. He is slowly piecing himself together from instinct and moral conviction and the increasingly loud sense that something was done to him that requires a response.
He materialised from almost nothing — a confused, furious force of nature with no context and enormous power. He is more powerful than a ghost his age should be by any normal measure, and for reasons he cannot access. He is gaining coherence. He is gaining memory. He is going to be a problem for someone, and that someone does not yet know who they are dealing with.

Choppy jet black bob. Not a style he chose — a wound his soul carries without remembering the wounding. In life his hair was very long. It was cut before his execution as a public humiliation.

Heels. Old-fashioned fancy medieval clothes — not quite the military regalia of his life, but something adjacent. Formal without being armoured.

Personality
Intact through death

Current mode
Sharp, snarky, quiet until he has something worth saying. He and Rhys bicker like siblings — he is the older sibling who finds Rhys's chaos offensive on a structural level and grudgingly endearing in practice.

Has processed this to the extent that a person with no memory of being alive can process it. He is not in denial. He is annoyed.
Goal
Once he understood he was murdered, the goal became clear without him having to find it. He wants the person responsible — or more specifically, what remains of them — to face a consequence. He and Rhys are looking for Cecil's modern lineage.
Relationships

The only person who can consistently perceive him. Started as resentful captivity on both sides. Has become something resembling a genuine dynamic — they bicker and neither admits to caring and both clearly do. Rhys is accidentally helping Florence remember how to be a person.
Everyone else
Cannot currently interact with most people. Is gaining power. This will change.
🔒 Who he was
Florence was a war general and the son of a king. He served under his own country's military and was one of the most effective field commanders of his era. He had a metal prosthetic leg, a lieutenant named Raphael who he had once sentenced to death and then adopted into his life, and a healer named Solstice who he fell in love with.
🔒 How he died
Cecil — his childhood friend turned king — had him publicly accused of treason, humiliated, and executed. His long jet black hair was cut off before the execution as a ritual degradation. Cecil's reasons involved Sol and the Solar Covenant.
🔒 Why his hair is that length
Cecil grabbed him by the hair and cut it before the execution. Florence does not remember Cecil or his death. His soul maintained the wound anyway. He materialises as Cecil left him.
🔒 Why he is so powerful
Solstice spent his remaining life imbuing Florence's grave with solar healing magic, hoping for a miracle that never came. A thousand years of accumulated grief-love is part of what makes Florence as powerful as he is. He has been marinating in Sol's love for ten centuries. Sol did bring him back. He just did not live to know it.
🔒 The recognition moment
Florence will eventually regain enough memory to recognise Cecil. His eyes were green. When he looks at Arwin — who has Cecil's green eyes, a family trait — something in him will recognise it before his conscious memory catches up. This is the moment everything converges.
🔒 What he does not know about Rhys
Rhys has feelings for Arwin. Arwin is Cecil's descendant. Florence is hunting Cecil's lineage. These three facts have not yet occupied the same space in his awareness simultaneously.

Vesper
Head of the Celestial Church. Over a thousand years old. The only being in this world who knows the full history. He is not human and should not be evaluated as one.
Both ErasCelestial ChurchAncientNon-HumanSealed for 1000 Years
Overview
Vesper predates most of what currently exists. He has been the head of the Celestial Church for longer than reliable records account for. He communes with stars. He reads and sometimes modifies the strings of fate. He has watched civilisations construct themselves and fail. He is the only living being who knows the true history of the world — what Sol was, what the Reclaimer mythology actually means, what the Lunars actually did, why the Solar Covenant really collapsed.
He does not volunteer this information.
He is not evil. He is not good. He is not benevolent or malevolent or kind or cruel. He is a being so far outside human moral categories that applying them is a category error — the way applying human morality to weather is a category error. Something happened to him across his thousand years of existence and the subsequent thousand years of sealed immobility that has made his grip on time and reality fractured. He sometimes confuses the present with the past. He sometimes mistakes people for other people. He carries what remains of the one person he has ever gotten along with.
Appearance
Height
Approximately 2.5 metres.
Quality
Uncanny valley. Smooth, offputting, mannequin-like. The face is too even, the skin too unmarked, the proportions subtly wrong in ways that register before the conscious mind can identify what is wrong.
Eyes
Two main eyes: huge, with slit pupils like a cat or snake. White scleras. A third eye on his forehead — this one moves independently of the other two. Its iris is near-colourless, almost entirely pupil.
Build
Grotesquely slender. Bends where he should not. Three joints instead of two in his limbs. Long bony fingers with long black nails.
Clothing
Endless layered purple robes. They have twinkling points of light in the fabric — actual stars, or something that behaves like them. Silver embroidery. The layers shift when he moves.
Hair
Long, dark, ashy-purple. Very long.
General impression
Wrong, in a way that is difficult to look at directly for long. Not frightening exactly, but deeply uncomfortable. The kind of presence that makes your hindbrain suggest you should be somewhere else.
Nature & Abilities
Category
Not human. Not a ghost, not a god in any traditional sense, not a demon. Something that exists in a category that human language does not have good words for. He communes with stars as entities — they communicate with him in ways that have given him information and capabilities that operate outside normal physical or magical frameworks.
Fate manipulation
Can interact with the strings of fate as written in the stars. Read them. In certain cases, modify them. This is not unlimited — it cost him significantly when he modified Cecil's bloodline at Sol's request, and he was at his most powerful then. In his current diminished state his capabilities are reduced.
Memory and time
Fractured. He occasionally confuses the present with the past. He sometimes calls people by the wrong name — specifically the name of someone he knew a thousand years ago. He keeps objects that belonged to people who are gone. His relationship to linear time is not the same as a human's.
Power level
Significantly diminished compared to the Fantasy Era. A thousand years of sealed immobility has taken something from him that has not fully returned. He may never be what he was.
The third eye
Moves independently. Sometimes seems to perceive things the other two eyes do not. Has a history that explains its near-colourless appearance.
Relationships
Sol (Solstice)
The one. The only person Vesper has ever genuinely gotten along with, across however many centuries he has existed. He developed something that functions like longing — not in a human sense, but in whatever sense is possible for what he is. He carries Sol's skull. He replaced his own third eye with Sol's damaged eye. He spent a thousand years of paralysis with Sol's eye in his skull looking at darkness.
Lysander
His current acolyte. Vesper's relationship to Lysander is not simple to characterise because Vesper's relationships to people are not simple to characterise. He does not see Lysander the way Lysander sees him.
Arwin
Allied with Arwin's church in the modern era. Their interactions are infrequent and strange. He sometimes calls Arwin by the wrong name.
The Lunars
Previously allied with them. Abandoned that alliance when Arwin came to power. This was not malice — it was a calculation, or something like one. He does not carry grudges in a human way.
🔒 The third eye explained
Vesper's third eye is Solstice's eye. Sol's own eye was damaged — blind or near-blind. Vesper replaced his third eye with it. A long time. Long enough for institutions to rise and fall. Long enough for people to be born and die and be born again for thirty or forty generations. Three major institutions: Solar Covenant, Lunar Covenant, Celestial Church. Smaller factions (the Forge, others) exist in the margins. No single institution has hegemony. Political power is distributed, contested, negotiated.

How the shift happened
Over a thousand years, gradually. The institution Sol built in grief expanded through the mechanisms that institutions expand — absorbing smaller powers, establishing legal authority, eliminating competitors or driving them underground. This was not Sol's intention. It is what institutions do when nobody stops them.
Distorted History
The principle
A thousand years is a long time. True history gets buried, rewritten, simplified, weaponised, and forgotten. What the modern world believes about its own origins is a mix of partial truth, deliberate distortion, mythological accretion, and genuine forgetting.
The Lunar persecution
The most visible example. The official reason is heresy. The actual historical reason is that Lunars helped seal Vesper during the Fantasy Era — an action that was arguably correct at the time and has been distorted into something unrecognisable over a millennium. The people being persecuted now had nothing to do with it.
The Abyssal Church's origin
The church's official history describes a divine revelation and the faithful who built an institution around it. The actual origin is a grieving man building something to survive in after the death of the person he loved. Both histories are true in different senses. Only one is taught.
The Solar Covenant
Almost completely erased. A thousand years of deliberate suppression has been effective. The modern world does not know what the Solar Covenant was, what it did, or that its founding figure became the Reclaimer they venerate.
Who knows the truth
Vesper. He was there. He knows everything — the founding of the Abyssal Church, who Sol was, what the Reclaimer actually means, why the Solar Covenant collapsed, what the Lunars actually did and why. He does not volunteer this information. He is the only living being who holds all of it.

Lunar Magic
Overview
The Lunar Covenant's tradition of magic centres on the relationship between the living and the dead, and on perception of things that are not fully in the physical world. Practitioners are identifiable by white hair and typically elf-like ears — these traits cannot be concealed.
Communication with the dead
Genuine communion with ghosts and other non-corporeal entities. Not metaphorical. Rhys actually hears and perceives Florence. This is not common. Most people cannot do it at all.
Seership
Perception beyond the ordinary. Sometimes prophetic — flashes of what is coming, or what has been. Sometimes just sensitive — an awareness of wrongness, of presence, of things that should not be there. Variable in reliability and specificity. Cannot be fully controlled.
Exorcism
Removal or containment of hostile entities. Practical work. Dangerous. Exhausting. The thing the Abyssal Church actually pays Rhys to do, dressed up as consultation.
The toll
None of this is free. Using Lunar abilities — especially extended contact with the dead or with hostile entities — degrades the practitioner's psychological state. Extended seership leaves marks. Extended contact with powerful ghosts like Florence is not neutral. Rhys manages this by limiting use and not telling anyone how much he is limiting.
The tradition
Lunar practice has frameworks for managing the toll — community support structures, limits on use, ways of recovering. These frameworks have largely not survived into the modern era. Modern Lunars who practice are largely managing this alone.
Solar Magic
Overview
The Solar Covenant's tradition of magic is associated with healing, light, and life. Practitioners are identifiable by golden blonde hair. The Covenant no longer exists as an institution, but the magic it represented was real.
Healing
Direct healing of physical injuries and illness. Sol as a healer is genuinely effective — his assignment to Florence is not ceremonial. Solar healing is not miracle-working but it is significantly beyond what ordinary medicine achieves.
Imbuing
Solar healing magic can be placed into locations and objects, persisting long after the practitioner has gone. This is not a short-term effect — it accumulates and strengthens over time rather than fading. The healing creek is the most significant example of this effect.
Life affinity
Solar practitioners have a general affinity with living things — growth, vitality, the continuation of life. This is in contrast to Lunar affinity with the space between life and death.
Light
Actual light manipulation appears to be within Solar capability, though the extent is not detailed.
Sol specifically
Has abilities that are not standard Solar practice — specifically in the direction of death magic. The Reclaimer abilities. Whether this is unique to him or latent in Solar tradition is a thread to develop.
Celestial Magic
Overview
The Celestial Church's tradition operates on a different scale from Solar or Lunar magic. It is concerned with fate, with the stars as entities, with forces that do not map onto human categories of good or harm.
Star communion
Vesper communicates with stars as living entities. They tell him things. He acts on what he learns. The nature of what stars communicate and how is not publicly documented.
Fate reading
The ability to perceive the strings of fate written in the stars — to see the trajectory of a person's destiny. Not perfectly, not with complete precision, but genuinely. This is what Celestial acolytes learn in lesser forms.
Fate modification
Rare, costly, enormous. Vesper modified Cecil's string of fate at Sol's request — physically altered the celestial notation of his destiny so it would apply to all descendants. This was a significant use of power even at Vesper's full strength. He cannot do this in his current diminished state.

Officially banned by Abyssal Church doctrine. Publicly considered profane, spiritually dangerous, legally prohibited.
What it actually is
Real and functional. Resurrection of the dead, communication with the dead, creation and manipulation of undead states. These things work.

The Abyssal Church bans necromancy. The Abyssal Church's Bishop is an undead created by a necromantic ritual. The Abyssal Church's founding figure was a practitioner of death magic. The Reclaimer, their central theological figure, is named after a death-magic concept. The contradiction is total.

Ghosts
Formation
When a person dies, something of them can persist. Not always. The conditions for ghost formation versus ordinary death are not fully understood.
Form
A ghost manifests as how they remember themselves at the moment of death — including psychological wounds that left marks so deep they shaped the self-image. Florence materialises without his prosthetic leg (which he felt made him lesser) and with his hair cut short (which was done to him in humiliation). He does not remember either of these things, but his soul does.
Power
Most ghosts are weak and diffuse. Power accrues over time, especially in proximity to magical sites. A ghost buried in a site continuously imbued with powerful Solar magic for a thousand years is going to be significantly stronger than average.
Coherence
Ghosts start diffuse — confused, reactive, not quite persons. Coherence is gained over time, through interaction, through proximity to the living, through the slow return of memory and self. Florence started as barely a coherent force of nature and is becoming, gradually, a person again.
Summoning
Can be performed through ritual. Technically necromancy. Technically banned. Rhys does it anyway. His summoning of Florence was accidental — he was performing a general ritual for personal gain near a site that turned out to be Florence's grave.
The magical site interaction
Ghost power and the properties of magical sites can interact. A ghost buried in a Solar-imbued site absorbs something from that imbuing. Florence being more powerful than he should be is related to Sol's thousand years of accumulated magic.
